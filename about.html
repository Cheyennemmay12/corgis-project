01 / The breed

Two Welsh originals.
One big spirit.

Corgis are compact herding dogs with alert minds, sturdy bodies, and a knack for making themselves part of everything.

A Welsh beginning

Made for the hills
and the herd.

For generations, Welsh farmers relied on corgis to move cattle and other livestock. Their low build helped them nip at heels and duck away from a kick; their energy and quick thinking made them capable workers across a long day.

The name refers to two separate breeds: the Pembroke Welsh Corgi and the Cardigan Welsh Corgi. They share a Welsh homeland and a herding heritage, but each has its own breed history and standard.

Two breeds, side by side

Pembroke & Cardigan

Both are active, intelligent companions. These are familiar distinctions, not a substitute for meeting an individual dog.

P

Pembroke Welsh Corgi

Often recognized by a fox-like outline, pointed ears, and a naturally short or docked tail. Pembrokes are spirited, sociable herders.

From Pembrokeshire, Wales
C

Cardigan Welsh Corgi

Typically has a long, brush-like tail, rounded ears, and a wider range of coat colors. Cardigans are adaptable, devoted, and alert.

From Cardiganshire, Wales

A bright, working mind

Curious, quick to learn, and happiest with a job to do, even if that job is learning a new trick.
